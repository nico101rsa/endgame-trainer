## The face-off

Two kings stand on the same line with one empty square between them. Whoever has to move must give way — kings can't step next to each other, so the mover loses the stand-off. The side NOT to move is said to have **the opposition**.

## Why it wins games

In king-and-pawn endings the kings fight over the squares in front of the pawn. With the opposition you force the other king aside and walk into the space he leaves. Without it, you're the one stepping aside.

Two working rules:

- **Attacking:** lead with your king, not your pawn. Push the pawn only when the pawn move itself hands the face-off back to your opponent — a spare tempo.
- **Defending:** plant your king in the pawn's path and mirror the enemy king. He slides, you slide.
