## The two verdicts

Nearly every king-and-pawn-versus-king position turns on one question: whose king controls the squares in front of the pawn?

- **Attacker's king in front of its pawn, two ranks ahead: winning**, no matter whose move. One rank ahead: winning only with the opposition. On the 6th rank in front of the pawn: always winning.
- **Defender's king in front of the pawn, holding the opposition: drawn.**

(Rook pawns break these rules — next lesson.)

## Winning: the escort

King leads, pawn follows. Sidestep to force the defender off the file, walk the pawn up under cover, and mind the finish: the pawn must reach its 7th rank **without giving check**. Then your king shields the promotion square and nothing can stop the queen.

## Drawing: straight back

Stay planted in the pawn's path and mirror the enemy king. When the pawn itself pushes you back, retreat **straight back on its file** — never diagonally. Done right, the pawn reaches the 7th only *with* check; you slide in front of it, and White's choice is stalemate or losing the pawn. The 7th rank tells you instantly who did it right: check = draw, no check = win.
