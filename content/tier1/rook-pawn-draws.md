## The drawish file

Rook pawns break the rules of the last lesson. Every other pawn has two neighbouring files to outflank through; the a- and h-pawns have one, and it's not enough. Two fortresses exist against a rook pawn that exist nowhere else on the board.

## Fortress one: sit in the corner

Get your defending king to the promotion corner — a8/b8 against an a-pawn, g1/h1 against an h-pawn — and nothing evicts it. Even with the attacking king perfectly placed, every try ends the same way: the pawn arrives and it's stalemate.

## Fortress two: wall him in

If the attacking king leads its own rook pawn — a7, a8, in front — seal it in from the c-file. One king on c7 covers b6, b7 and b8 all at once; shuffle c7–c8 and the attacker's king never comes out. His own pawn bricks up the last square.

## When it still wins

The rook pawn wins only when the attacking king reaches the square **beside** the corner first — b7 against Black's king, shielding a8 from the side without ever standing in front of the pawn. Beside escorts; in front entombs.
