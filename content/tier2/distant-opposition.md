## The face-off, stretched

Tier 1's opposition rule works at range. Kings on the same line with **one, three, or five** empty squares between them: whoever must move gives way. An odd gap is opposition; an even gap belongs to the mover, who can make it odd.

Distant opposition is a promise, not a prize — it only pays out when it shrinks to the direct face-off. As the kings walk toward each other, keep the gap odd; when it reaches one square, you're back in Tier 1.

## What the fight is actually about

The attacking king wants the squares two ranks in front of his pawn. The defender's whole job is to be standing guard when the attacker arrives — facing him, gap odd, covering the entry squares.

- **One square too far back, or one file to the side, and the attacking king slips past.** The drills below are all about arriving on exactly the right square.
- A spare pawn move can still steal the face-off — that Tier 1 trick doesn't stop working just because the kings started far apart.
